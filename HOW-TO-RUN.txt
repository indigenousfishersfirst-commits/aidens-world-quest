Aiden's World Quest, Expedition Edition - how to run it

ON A PC (works with no internet)
1. Unzip this folder.
2. Double-click index.html. It opens in Chrome or Edge.
3. Choose "Start with music and voice". Use the full-screen button at the top, or press F11.
4. Music is now soft on purpose. Turn the volume up a little if needed, or use the sliders in the settings (the sliders icon at the top right). Music, place sounds, narration and effects are all in the folder.

ON AN ANDROID TABLET
Open the game's web link in Chrome, open the menu (three dots), and choose "Add to Home screen"
or "Install app". It then opens full screen and keeps working offline after the first visit.
Check that the media volume is up and the tablet is not on silent.

KEEPING PROGRESS
- The game saves automatically on the device and browser it is played in. When Aiden returns, his keys,
  Pokemon friends, atlas pages, words, journal and settings are all still there.
- Each device saves separately. To move to another device, open "Save & backup", download the private
  backup file or copy the backup code (AWQ3), and restore it on the other device.
- Clearing browser data or private browsing can erase the local save. Download a backup now and then.
- Older AWQ1 and AWQ2 codes can still be restored.
- Up to six local explorers can have their own camp on one device.

WHAT IS NEW IN THIS VERSION: THE EXPEDITION
- Every scene now flows the same way: Explore (three short story cards), Experiment (a hands-on Field Lab),
  Decode (one plain question) and Discover (a Nature Dex card, a golden key and a "Next scene" button).
  There is no path to choose any more, so the game keeps moving on its own.
- 18 Field Labs, one per scene, built around science and the outdoors: tides and wet lines, salmon and forests,
  habitats, river currents, rice and food engineering, wind, camp shade, arches, photosynthesis, mapping a river,
  soaking lentils, placing weights, patterns, lenses, plant roots, coastal clean-up sorting and more. Each lab is a
  simplified game model and says so. Wrong tries give a friendly nudge, never a penalty.
- Questions are shorter and say what they are looking for: WHERE, WHO, WHAT, WHICH or HOW, with a one-line hint
  and a "Look back at the story" button. All 18 stories and 36 questions were rewritten as small mysteries with
  new narration (144 new voice clips).
- Nature Dex: every discovery is collected, with explorer ranks and field gear unlocked at 1, 3, 6, 9, 12 and
  18 discoveries. Gear powers the Trail Dash.
- Explorer TV: 40 short YouTube clips about real places, people, animals, things and food. A clip loads only
  when Aiden taps "Load video". It needs internet. The offline PC copy works fully without videos.
  Clips from Indigenous and cultural sources are marked "Watch together" for a grown-up to preview.
  A grown-up can switch Explorer TV off in For grown-ups, where a library preview is also available.
  Nothing from the game is sent to YouTube, but loading the player shares connection data with that service.

EARLIER ADDITIONS (SOUNDS AND GRANDMA EDITION)
- Softer music. The music starts at a low level and gets quieter still whenever someone is speaking.
  A new setting, Audio mix, has separate sliders for music, place sounds, voice and effects.
- Place sounds. Each country has its own background (shore, temple garden, Roman square, river and market,
  mountain wind, harbour) with small sounds that come and go: gulls, birds, frogs, boat and ferry horns,
  bicycle and tram bells, church and temple bells, scooters, car horns, camel bells, panpipes, a llama,
  kookaburras and cockatoos. Each story card also brings in a sound that fits it, and a few stories add rain.
  Turn them off with the switch called Place sounds.
- Music that follows the play. In Trail Dash and the friendship battles, extra layers of music fade in and the
  pace rises as time runs low, as a chain grows or when an obstacle is close. It settles after a bump.
  Calm mode keeps it gentle. Reading, word and matching games stay quiet and have no timers.
- Bigger wins. Finishing a key, building a word, a personal best and a friendship badge each bring a
  brighter fanfare and a burst of stars. A new friend or a badge gets the biggest one. Switch off
  Playful motion to remove the bursts.
- Grandma. All narration now uses one warm, steady, grandmotherly voice. She also gives short tips,
  encouragement and cheers, with a caption bubble that can be closed. Turn her tips off with the switch called Grandma's tips.
- Harder words. The Word Forge now has 36 words. The first 18 are the starters. A second pack of 18
  longer Trail words opens after six starter words are built on their own, and then grows with the keys.
  Trail words include extra decoy tiles. A grown-up can open all 36 in the For grown-ups page.

WHAT IS IN THIS EDITION
- Stories: three read-aloud story cards, a committed answer, and then the evidence sentence that supports it.
  Wrong answers lead to a review of the clue, not to a punishment. Fast guessing costs trail points
  and earns no bonus XP, and clicking through the choices earns nothing.
- Tiers: Scout (details first), Explorer (inference first) and Detective (two questions per story).
  The game can adjust the tier after recent stories.
- Field Labs and the Nature Dex replace the old path choice (see the Expedition notes above).
- Where in the world next: the atlas case in basecamp links clues from the stories.
- Trail Dash: stars, gems, leaves and clocks add points. Obstacles cost points and time. A shield and a
  magnet power are earned from Field Lab discoveries. Calm mode slows the trail.
- Word forge: hear a word, build it from tiles or type it, and try optional finger tracing.
  Hearing a word is always free.
- Clues to revisit: earlier clues return after a day, three days, a week and later.
- Growth journal: a feeling, a thank-you and something Aiden cannot do yet.
- Family field book: off-screen missions for outside and at home. Grown-ups can add their own.
- For grown-ups: a progress view, tier and text controls, break reminders, local friends and custom missions.
- Reading supports: larger text, roomier lines, a focus window, a line guide, read-aloud and optional device voice.

HONEST LIMITS
All music and place sounds were made for this game with a sound synthesizer. They are not field recordings.
The narration is a computer-generated voice that was checked by automated tools only.
This is a learning game. It is not a treatment or a test of dyslexia, ADHD or reading level. Its supports are
inspired by structured-literacy, classroom-attention and spaced-practice guidance. Talk with a teacher or
specialist about Aiden's own learning plan.

SAVING YOUR PLACE
Progress saves by itself on each device and browser. Tap "Save my place" any time, and the next visit opens with "Continue where I left off".
Saves stay with the link and browser you use, so keep opening the game the same way on each device (same link, same browser).
To move progress to another device, use "Save & backup" to download a backup file, then load it on the other device.
